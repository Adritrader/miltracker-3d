Israel–Gaza Conflict:
Live Military Tracking & Intelligence

Overview

The Israel–Gaza conflict escalated dramatically following the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, which triggered a sustained Israeli military campaign in the Gaza Strip and set off a wider regional confrontation involving Hezbollah in Lebanon, Iranian-backed militias in Iraq and Syria, the Houthi movement in Yemen, and direct exchanges between Israel and Iran. The conflict has evolved through multiple phases of ground offensives, temporary ceasefires, hostage-release negotiations, and periodic escalations that have repeatedly drawn in regional actors.

Israel's military campaign has focused on dismantling Hamas's military infrastructure in Gaza, including its extensive tunnel network, while contending with international pressure over civilian casualties and humanitarian conditions in the densely populated territory. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) have conducted repeated ground incursions, air campaigns, and targeted strikes against militant leadership, while Hamas and allied factions have continued rocket and mortar fire into Israeli territory.

Ground Operations in Gaza

IDF ground operations in Gaza have proceeded in phases targeting specific sectors — northern Gaza, Gaza City, Khan Yunis, and Rafah along the Egyptian border. Israeli forces have used combined-arms tactics involving Merkava main battle tanks, D9 armoured bulldozers, and infantry supported by close air support, while militant factions have relied on anti-tank guided missiles, improvised explosive devices, and the extensive underground tunnel network to sustain resistance.

The Philadelphi Corridor along the Gaza–Egypt border and the Netzarim Corridor bisecting the strip have been key axes of Israeli military control, used to interdict smuggling routes and divide Gaza into separately controlled sectors. Repeated humanitarian pauses and ceasefire agreements — including hostage-for-prisoner exchange frameworks brokered by Qatar, Egypt, and the United States — have punctuated the fighting without producing a lasting resolution.

Air Defence: Iron Dome and Multi-Layered Systems

Israel's air defence architecture is built around a multi-layered system designed to intercept threats at different ranges and altitudes:

  • Iron Dome — Short-range system optimised against rockets, artillery, and mortars fired from Gaza and Lebanon; achieves a high interception rate against short-range unguided projectiles.
  • David's Sling — Mid-range interceptor for cruise missiles and larger rockets, used against Hezbollah's more advanced arsenal.
  • Arrow 2 / Arrow 3 — Long-range and exo-atmospheric interceptors developed specifically to counter ballistic missile threats from Iran, used operationally during direct Iranian missile barrages.
  • THAAD — A US Terminal High Altitude Area Defense battery was deployed to Israel with American crews to reinforce ballistic missile defence during periods of heightened Iranian threat.

Direct Iranian missile and drone attacks on Israel in April and October 2024 — involving hundreds of ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and Shahed-type drones — were intercepted at high rates by this layered system working alongside US, UK, French, and Jordanian assets, though some ballistic missiles did penetrate the defensive umbrella.

Regional Spillover: Lebanon and Hezbollah

Hezbollah opened a parallel front along the Israel–Lebanon border in solidarity with Hamas, leading to sustained cross-border exchanges of fire that escalated into a full Israeli air and ground campaign in Lebanon during 2024. Israeli strikes killed senior Hezbollah leadership and degraded significant portions of its rocket and missile arsenal, while Hezbollah continued firing rockets, drones, and anti-tank missiles into northern Israel, displacing tens of thousands of residents on both sides of the border.

A ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hezbollah, brokered with US and French mediation, has faced repeated violations and only partially restored calm to the border region.

OSINT Tracking Note: Israeli Air Force fighters, ISR aircraft, and tanker aircraft operating over Israel and the Eastern Mediterranean are frequently visible via ADS-B where transponders remain active, though many sorties over Gaza and Lebanon occur with transponders disabled for operational security. Naval assets including Sa'ar-class corvettes patrolling Gaza's coast and the Mediterranean are tracked via AIS when transponders are active.

Iran–Israel Direct Confrontation

The conflict has repeatedly drawn Iran into direct military confrontation with Israel — a marked departure from decades of proxy warfare. Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear and military facilities, and Iranian ballistic missile and drone barrages against Israeli territory, represent the most significant direct state-on-state exchanges between the two countries in the conflict's history, raising sustained concern about further escalation involving Iran's nuclear programme and regional proxy network.
