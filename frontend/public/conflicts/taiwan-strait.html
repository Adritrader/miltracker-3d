Taiwan Strait Military Tensions:
Live Tracking & Intelligence

Updated: July 2026 ● Elevated Tension Source: Public OSINT

Overview

The Taiwan Strait — the 180-kilometre-wide body of water separating mainland China from Taiwan — represents the single most likely flashpoint for a great-power military confrontation in the 21st century. The People's Republic of China (PRC) claims Taiwan as a breakaway province to be reunified by force if necessary, while the island's democratically elected government maintains de facto independence and its own military forces.

The United States does not formally recognise Taiwan's independence but maintains strategic ambiguity, provides defensive weapons under the Taiwan Relations Act (1979), and has repeatedly stated it would defend Taiwan in the event of a Chinese attack. The combination of Beijing's stated unification timeline, ongoing PLA military modernisation, and increasing US-China geopolitical competition has made the strait one of the world's most closely watched military zones.

PLAAF Air Incursions

People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) incursions into Taiwan's Air Defence Identification Zone (ADIZ) have become a near-daily occurrence since 2020. Taiwan's Ministry of National Defence publishes daily reports of PLA aircraft crossing the median line of the strait or entering the ADIZ's south-western sector — a pattern designed to normalise PLA presence, exhaust Taiwan's fighter pilot roster, and gather intelligence on Taiwan's air defence response protocols.

Aircraft types regularly observed in ADIZ incursions include:

  • J-10C Vigorous Dragon — Fourth-generation multirole fighter equipped with PL-15 beyond-visual-range missiles; forms the bulk of southern PLAAF fighter strength.
  • J-11B/BS Flanker — Heavy air superiority fighter based on the Russian Su-27 design; carries long-range air-to-air missiles for fleet defence.
  • J-16 — Advanced multirole strike fighter capable of carrying an extensive ordnance load; also appears in an electronic warfare variant (J-16D).
  • H-6K/N Bomber — Updated variant of the Soviet Tu-16 design; capable of launching CJ-20 air-launched cruise missiles with 1,500+ km range.
  • KJ-500 AEW&C — Airborne early warning and control aircraft providing battle management capability during large exercises.
  • Y-8/Y-9 Variants — Electronic intelligence (ELINT), submarine patrol (ASW), and psychological warfare variants regularly probe Taiwan's electronic environment.

PLAN Naval Activity

The People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) has undergone the fastest naval build-up of any country in the modern era, commissioning more warships in a single decade than most navies possess in total. PLAN carrier strike groups — centred on the Liaoning (Type 001), Shandong (Type 002), and Fujian (Type 003, China's first catapult-equipped carrier) — now conduct regular exercises in the Philippine Sea east of Taiwan, simulating the kind of fleet action that would be required for a blockade or amphibious assault.

AIS tracking on LiveWar3D captures PLAN surface vessels that have their transponders active, primarily during transits in peacetime conditions. During military exercises, the PLAN frequently operates under emissions control (EMCON), meaning vessels are not visible via AIS. The presence or absence of tracked PLAN vessels can itself be an intelligence indicator: a sudden disappearance of previously tracked ships may indicate the start of a major exercise.

US Navy Response

The United States Navy conducts regular carrier strike group (CSG) patrols in the Indo-Pacific, explicitly asserting freedom of navigation under international law. US Seventh Fleet, headquartered at Yokosuka, Japan, maintains continuous naval presence in the Western Pacific. Key US capabilities in the region include:

  • Multiple carrier strike groups based around Nimitz and Gerald R. Ford-class supercarriers, each carrying 70–90 aircraft including F/A-18E/F Super Hornets, EA-18G Growler electronic attack aircraft, E-2D Advanced Hawkeye AEW aircraft, and carrier-based MH-60R maritime helicopters.
  • Los Angeles and Virginia-class nuclear attack submarines (SSNs) operating from Guam and Yokosuka, providing persistent subsurface intelligence collection and strike capability.
  • Arleigh Burke-class Aegis destroyers equipped with SM-2, SM-3, and SM-6 missiles for layered air and missile defence.
  • B-52H Stratofortress and B-2 Spirit bombers operating from Andersen Air Force Base, Guam, and occasional deployments to Misawa and Kadena Air Bases.
Tracking Note: US carrier strike groups frequently broadcast AIS signals when operating in international waters away from sensitive operations. Their positions are visible on LiveWar3D when active transponders are detected. During heightened exercises or sensitive operations, vessels may reduce their electronic emissions, making them invisible to AIS-based tracking — their absence from the plot is notable in itself.

Japanese and Allied Responses

Japan's Air Self-Defence Force (ASDF) scrambled fighters at record rates in recent years to intercept Russian and Chinese aircraft approaching the Japanese archipelago. Japan's proximity to Taiwan (the southernmost Ryukyu Islands lie just 100 kilometres from Taiwan's eastern coast) makes it a frontline state in any cross-strait contingency.

Japan has dramatically increased defence spending, announced the acquisition of long-range Tomahawk cruise missiles and domestically developed Type 12 anti-ship missiles with extended range, and revised its National Security Strategy to allow counter-strike capabilities for the first time since the Second World War. Kadena Air Base in Okinawa, one of the most capable US air power nodes in the Pacific, would be a primary staging hub for any US response to a Taiwan contingency.

Australia's AUKUS nuclear submarine agreement commits Australia to acquiring nuclear-powered attack submarines in the 2030s, significantly extending allied undersea presence in the Indo-Pacific. South Korea, though focused primarily on the Korean Peninsula, also monitors Chinese military activity with concern.
